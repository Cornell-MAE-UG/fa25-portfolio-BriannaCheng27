The homework assignment I found most valuable was Problem 1, which focused on heat transfer through a pin fin and a fin array. In this problem, I had to apply several different concepts from class, including temperature distribution along a fin, fin efficiency and effectiveness, and thermal resistance. Working through the steps helped me understand how heat moves through a fin and how the fin interacts with the surrounding air through convection. It was also helpful to see how the equations we learned in lecture actually connect to a physical system rather than just being formulas to memorize.

One part that stood out to me was when we analyzed the full fin array and then compared the heater temperature with and without the heat sink. This made it clear how much adding fins can improve heat dissipation by increasing the surface area for convection. Before doing this problem, I understood the idea of fins in theory, but this assignment helped me see quantitatively how they reduce temperature in a real system. I think this will be useful in the future because many engineering systems rely on heat sinks or similar cooling methods, and being able to model them with thermal resistance networks and fin analysis will help when designing or evaluating thermal management solutions.
